First contact resolution (FCR) is the percentage of customer issues fully resolved during a single interaction, with no callback, transfer, or repeat contact required. Why does FCR matter for customer retention? Because the cross-industry average sits close to 70%, and world-class performance — 80% or higher — is reached by only about 5% of contact centers ([SQM Group, 2025](https://www.sqmgroup.com/resources/library/blog/call-center-fcr-benchmark-2024-results-by-industry)). Every point below that average is a quantifiable early-warning signal that a share of those customers are at elevated risk of leaving — not just a soft service-quality complaint.

## What First Contact Resolution Actually Measures

FCR answers one specific question: did the customer's issue get solved the first time they reached out, without needing to come back? Zendesk defines it as the share of support interactions closed on the first attempt ([Zendesk, 2026](https://www.zendesk.com/blog/customer-experience/retention/first-contact-resolution-friend-foe-frenemy/)). If you're wondering how to calculate FCR, the first contact resolution formula is straightforward: FCR (%) equals the number of interactions resolved on first contact, divided by total interactions, multiplied by 100 ([Tollanis, 2026](https://tollanis.com/blog/first-call-resolution-benchmarks-formula-and-ways-to-improve-it)).

What counts as "resolved" matters more than the formula itself. A ticket marked closed by an agent isn't automatically first-contact resolved if the customer has to reopen it, call back about the same issue, or escalate later. Gartner's guidance on measuring FCR warns that many teams think they're tracking first call resolution accurately but are only capturing channel-specific resolution — missing repeat contacts that happen through a different channel days later ([Gartner, 2024](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/5232563)). A defensible FCR figure has to follow the customer's full journey, not just one ticket's status field.

## The FCR Benchmarks That Matter: By Industry and By Channel

What is a good first contact resolution rate? It depends heavily on industry and channel, which is why a single FCR benchmark can be misleading without context. SQM Group's benchmarking of over 500 North American contact centers puts the aggregated cross-industry average FCR at roughly 70%, with performance ranging from about 43% to 88% depending on sector and complexity ([SQM Group, 2025](https://www.sqmgroup.com/resources/library/blog/call-center-fcr-benchmark-2024-results-by-industry)).

**First contact resolution rate by industry:**

| Industry | Average FCR |
|---|---|
| Financial Services | 78% |
| Insurance | 74% |
| Utilities | 73% |
| Healthcare | 71% |
| Retail / E-Commerce | 65–75% |

*Source: [SQM Group / Tollanis, 2025 industry benchmarking data (Tollanis, 2026)](https://tollanis.com/blog/first-call-resolution-benchmarks-formula-and-ways-to-improve-it)*

Channel matters just as much as industry when you're benchmarking first call resolution. Voice remains the highest-FCR channel because real-time dialogue lets agents clarify and resolve in the moment, while asynchronous channels like email and self-service naturally trail:

- Voice (inbound): 74% average FCR — highest of any channel, driven by real-time back-and-forth.
- Live chat: 72% average FCR — close to voice, aided by co-browsing and screen-sharing capability.
- Email: 62% average FCR — lower because asynchronous exchanges commonly require multiple messages before resolution.
- Self-service / IVR: 55% average FCR — lower resolution rate, but near-zero cost, so containment still carries value regardless of the FCR figure.

## Why FCR Predicts Retention Risk, Not Just Satisfaction

Does first contact resolution affect churn? The connection between FCR and customer retention runs through a specific, measurable chain: unresolved first contacts don't just annoy customers — they materially depress satisfaction and elevate the odds a customer leaves. SQM Group's research, cited by netsapiens, found that customers whose issue was resolved on the first contact reported 86% satisfaction, compared with only 42% satisfaction among customers who had to call back about the same issue — a nearly 2x satisfaction gap tied directly to one repeat contact ([netsapiens, 2023](https://www.netsapiens.com/first-contact-resolution-the-customer-service-kpi-businesses-must-focus-on/)).

![Comparison infographic showing 86% satisfaction when resolved on first contact versus 42% satisfaction after a repeat contact](/assets/images/September%202026/fcr-retention-risk-figure-02-repeat-contact-cost.webp){: loading="lazy" width="1200" height="654"}
*A single repeat contact roughly halves reported satisfaction — the mechanism behind FCR's link to churn. Source: SQM Group, via netsapiens.com.*
{: .figure-caption }

This is where FCR stops being a soft "quality" metric and becomes a leading indicator of FCR and customer retention risk together. Tollanis, citing Gartner's 2024 research, reports that customers who must recontact a company about the same issue face roughly 4x higher churn risk than customers whose problem was solved the first time ([Tollanis, 2026](https://tollanis.com/blog/first-call-resolution-benchmarks-formula-and-ways-to-improve-it)). SQM Group's broader research also identifies a close, near 1:1 relationship between improvements in FCR and improvements in CSAT — meaning FCR doesn't just correlate with satisfaction, it functionally moves it ([Tollanis, 2026](https://tollanis.com/blog/first-call-resolution-benchmarks-formula-and-ways-to-improve-it)).

For a CX or support leader, that turns FCR into a forward-looking metric rather than a backward-looking report card. A satisfaction survey tells you how a customer felt about a resolved interaction. FCR tells you, in real time, how many customers are quietly being pushed toward the recontact-and-frustration cycle that precedes cancellation, chargebacks, or silent churn — the exact retention-risk signal a CX leader needs before it shows up in a subscription-cancellation dashboard weeks later.

## What Drives FCR Down — and What Actually Raises It

Low FCR is rarely an individual agent's fault. It's usually a process and infrastructure problem: agents who lack authority to resolve an issue without escalating, knowledge bases that are incomplete or out of date, and routing systems that send a customer to the wrong queue on the first attempt. Callnovo's own frontline-support team has documented this directly — one agent's account of a 30-minute call with "nothing left to fix" showed that a repeat issue was actually a signal of a missing internal process, not a bad customer or an undertrained agent ([Callnovo Contact Center, 2026a](https://callnovo.ai/blog/frontline-support-reality/)).

The levers that reliably raise any FCR benchmark are well-documented across CX research and consistent with what that internal account illustrates:

- Agent authority: giving frontline agents the ability to approve refunds, adjustments, or exceptions on the spot, rather than escalating for approval.
- Unified knowledge and case history access: agents need to see a customer's full interaction history in one place, not stitched together across disconnected tools, to avoid asking the customer to repeat context.
- AI-assisted triage: routing a query to the right specialist channel or knowledge source on the first attempt, rather than relying on a generic first-line queue.
- Structured escalation design: a clear, fast internal path to a specialist for complex cases, so "first contact" can still mean a single resolved episode even if a specialist gets involved partway through.
- Continuous QA scoring: auditing a meaningful share of interactions for whether the issue was actually resolved, not just whether the ticket was marked closed.

## FAQ

**What is first contact resolution (FCR)?**
FCR is the percentage of customer issues fully resolved during a customer's first interaction with support, with no callback, transfer, or follow-up needed for the same issue.

**What is a good first contact resolution rate?**
Industry research classifies 70–79% as a good FCR rate and 80% or higher as world-class; the cross-industry average sits close to 70%, and only around 5% of contact centers consistently reach world-class ([SQM Group, 2025](https://www.sqmgroup.com/resources/library/blog/call-center-fcr-benchmark-2024-results-by-industry)).

**How do you calculate first contact resolution?**
FCR equals the number of issues resolved on the first contact divided by the total number of customer interactions, multiplied by 100 ([Tollanis, 2026](https://tollanis.com/blog/first-call-resolution-benchmarks-formula-and-ways-to-improve-it)).

**Does first contact resolution actually predict customer churn?**
Yes; research shows a close correlation between FCR and CSAT, and customers who must recontact support about the same issue report satisfaction at roughly half the rate of those whose issue was resolved on first contact, which elevates churn risk ([netsapiens, 2023](https://www.netsapiens.com/first-contact-resolution-the-customer-service-kpi-businesses-must-focus-on/); [Tollanis, 2026](https://tollanis.com/blog/first-call-resolution-benchmarks-formula-and-ways-to-improve-it)).

**Which industries have the lowest FCR rates?**
Complex, escalation-heavy categories like technical support and telecom tend to run lower than retail, e-commerce, and financial services, largely due to third-party dependencies and multi-step troubleshooting ([Tollanis, 2026](https://tollanis.com/blog/first-call-resolution-benchmarks-formula-and-ways-to-improve-it)).

**Can outsourcing customer support improve FCR?**
It can, when the outsourcing model is structured around resolution accountability rather than seat-time; Callnovo's Pay per Resolution service, for example, contracts on a 90%+ FCR SLA with performance credits if targets are missed ([Callnovo Contact Center, 2026d](https://callnovo.ai/services/pay-per-resolution)).
