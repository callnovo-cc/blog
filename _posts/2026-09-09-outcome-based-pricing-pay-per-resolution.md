***Outcome-based pricing shifts the core billing question from staffed hours to resolved outcomes — but only if "resolved" is defined the same way by both sides.***
{: .article-hero-caption }

> **TL;DR.** Pay-per-resolution pricing means paying only when a customer's issue is actually resolved, not for staffed hours, agent headcount, or handled contacts that go nowhere — and what counts as a resolution in customer service pricing is exactly where the real economics of this model live.

<!--more-->

It replaces the traditional per-FTE or per-hour outsourcing model with a per-outcome one: if the issue doesn't get fixed, the ticket isn't billed. The catch most buyers miss is that "resolved" is not a universal term; every vendor defines it differently, which is why outcome-based customer support pricing requires reading the fine print before comparing headline rates.

## Why Outcome-Based Pricing Is Gaining Ground in 2026

Outcome-based pricing has moved from a niche BPO offering to a mainstream conversation across AI and enterprise software in 2026, largely because AI agents made per-outcome billing operationally practical for the first time. SaaS and CX vendors are actively shifting away from flat per-seat and per-hour billing toward usage- and outcome-based models as buyers push back on paying for idle capacity ([Softwareseni, 2026](https://www.softwareseni.com/saas-pricing-is-shifting-from-per-seat-to-usage-and-outcome-what-changes-at-your-next-renewal/)). Executives researching this shift are frequently searching a specific question: is pay-per-resolution cheaper than per-agent pricing? The honest answer is: it depends entirely on ticket volume predictability and how the vendor defines a countable outcome ([Dholakia, 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/outcome-based-pricing-practice-what-outcomes-ai-really-utpal-dholakia-ihazc)).

The practical driver is cost transparency. Traditional per-FTE outsourcing bills for headcount regardless of whether tickets get resolved on the first contact or bounce repeatedly; outcome-based models tie the invoice directly to work that actually closes a customer's issue ([CX Foundation, 2026](https://cxfoundation.com/blog/outcome-based-pricing)). For companies with seasonal or unpredictable support volume, this converts a fixed labor cost into a variable one that scales with demand rather than staffing plans ([Callnovo, 2026a](https://callnovo.ai/pricing/)).

## How a "Resolution" Gets Defined and Measured

Definitions vary by vendor, and this variance is the single biggest source of billing disputes in outcome-based contracts. Zendesk: a ticket counts as an Automated Resolution if the customer does not make a repeat contact within 72 hours of the AI's response ([Softwareseni, 2026](https://www.softwareseni.com/saas-pricing-is-shifting-from-per-seat-to-usage-and-outcome-what-changes-at-your-next-renewal/)).

- Salesforce: requires explicit customer confirmation that the issue was solved before counting the resolution ([Flexprice, 2025](https://flexprice.io/blog/outcome-based-pricing))
- Fin (Intercom): counts satisfaction-survey-confirmed or no-recontact resolutions within a defined window ([valentinaincognito.com, 2026](https://valentinaincognito.com/blog/bpo-pricing-models))
- Callnovo's pay-per-resolution model: counts a resolution only when the customer's stated issue is closed and no repeat contact on the same issue occurs within an agreed window, verified through QA sampling ([Callnovo, 2026b](https://callnovo.ai/services/pay-per-resolution/))

![Governance scorecard infographic showing five hybrid customer-support quality dimensions]({{ "/September%202026/assets/images/outcome-based-pricing-pay-per-resolution-figure-01.webp" | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy" width="1200" height="675"}

*Governance scorecard dimensions — resolution quality, repeat-contact rate, escalation quality, satisfaction, and QA coverage — form the auditable backbone of any outcome-based contract.*

A frequent buyer question here is how do BPOs define a resolution in a way that can be independently audited rather than taken on faith. The answer, per current market practice, is a written resolution definition in the statement of work plus a shared QA sampling method both parties can access ([KORIX, 2026](https://korixinc.com/learning-center/ai-pricing-models-2026)). Contracts that leave "resolution" undefined or vendor-controlled without audit rights are the leading cause of outcome-pricing disputes reported across 2026 case reviews ([CX Foundation, 2026](https://cxfoundation.com/blog/outcome-based-pricing)).

## Economics, Risks, and Trade-offs: Pay Per Resolution vs. Per FTE

The core trade-off in pay per resolution vs per FTE pricing is predictability versus alignment. Per-FTE pricing gives buyers a fixed, forecastable monthly cost regardless of ticket complexity or volume swings, but it does not reward the vendor for speed or first-contact resolution ([Callnovo, 2026c](https://callnovo.com/call-center-costs-2025-worth-it-or-waste/)). Outcome-based pricing aligns vendor incentives directly with customer outcomes, but it introduces variability: a spike in complex, hard-to-resolve tickets can raise the effective per-resolution rate even though the headline number looks lower ([Dholakia, 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/outcome-based-pricing-practice-what-outcomes-ai-really-utpal-dholakia-ihazc)).

![Side-by-side comparison of a poor AI-to-human handoff with disconnected context versus a good handoff with a full context card]({{ "/September%202026/assets/images/outcome-based-pricing-pay-per-resolution-figure-02.webp" | relative_url }}){: loading="lazy" width="1200" height="675"}

*AI-to-human handoff quality directly determines whether a resolution counts on the first attempt or triggers a costly repeat contact.*

Risk allocation shifts too. Under per-FTE pricing, the buyer absorbs the cost of unresolved or bounced tickets since agents are paid regardless of outcome. Under outcome-based pricing, that risk moves to the vendor, which is precisely why vendors define "resolution" conservatively and why buyers must negotiate the definition rather than accept a boilerplate one ([KORIX, 2026](https://korixinc.com/learning-center/ai-pricing-models-2026)). Gaming the metric is a real risk: a vendor incentivized purely by resolution count may over-optimize for fast case closure rather than durable fixes, so pairing outcome pricing with a repeat-contact-rate cap protects against that failure mode ([CX Foundation, 2026](https://cxfoundation.com/blog/outcome-based-pricing)).

## 2026 Per-Resolution Rate Benchmarks Across the Market

Rate benchmarks vary sharply by ticket complexity and vendor category. AI-native platforms quoting pure automated resolutions report rates well below traditional per-agent equivalents for simple, high-volume issues, while BPOs blending AI triage with human escalation quote materially higher per-resolution rates for complex cases requiring judgment ([Flexprice, 2025](https://flexprice.io/blog/outcome-based-pricing)). This is the practical answer to outcome-based pricing vs per-hour outsourcing 2026: the model that wins depends on what share of a company's ticket mix is simple and automatable versus complex and human-dependent ([valentinaincognito.com, 2026](https://valentinaincognito.com/blog/bpo-pricing-models)).

| Decision factor | Favors pay-per-resolution | Favors per-FTE / per-hour |
|---|---|---|
| Ticket volume predictability | Volatile, seasonal, or spiky volume | Stable, forecastable volume |
| Ticket complexity mix | Majority simple, automatable issues | Majority complex, judgment-heavy issues |
| Internal QA maturity | Can audit resolution definitions and sampling | Limited capacity to audit outcome claims |
| Cost-control priority | Wants cost to scale with actual outcomes | Wants fixed, forecastable monthly spend |

## FAQ

**What is pay-per-resolution pricing in customer support outsourcing?**

Pay-per-resolution pricing bills only for tickets that meet an agreed definition of "resolved," instead of billing for staffed hours or headcount. The client and vendor agree in advance on what counts as a resolution and how it's measured ([Callnovo, 2026b](https://callnovo.ai/services/pay-per-resolution/)).

**What counts as a resolution in customer service pricing?**

Definitions vary by vendor: Zendesk counts a resolution if the customer doesn't recontact within 72 hours; Salesforce requires explicit customer confirmation; Fin counts satisfaction-confirmed or no-recontact resolutions ([Softwareseni, 2026](https://www.softwareseni.com/saas-pricing-is-shifting-from-per-seat-to-usage-and-outcome-what-changes-at-your-next-renewal/)). Buyers should get the exact definition in writing before signing.

**Is pay-per-resolution cheaper than per-agent pricing?**

It depends on ticket volume predictability and complexity mix. Pay-per-resolution tends to cost less for high-volume, simple, automatable tickets, while per-agent pricing can be more economical for complex, judgment-heavy support where AI resolution rates are lower ([Dholakia, 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/outcome-based-pricing-practice-what-outcomes-ai-really-utpal-dholakia-ihazc)).

**How do BPOs define a resolution for billing purposes?**

Reputable BPOs put the resolution definition and QA sampling method directly in the statement of work, with audit rights for the client, rather than leaving the term to unilateral vendor interpretation ([KORIX, 2026](https://korixinc.com/learning-center/ai-pricing-models-2026)).
