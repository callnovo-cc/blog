## Test purpose

This short post validates the metadata chain behind a governed AI customer support article. The test separates the human author, **Vince Lupe**, from the publishing organization, **Callnovo Contact Center**, while retaining a complete Article schema, social-share metadata, a responsive hero image, and a visible FAQ that matches the accompanying FAQPage JSON-LD.

The operating principle is the same one used in AI customer support governance: separate responsibilities clearly. Automation may handle repeatable, low-risk work; human agents own exceptions and judgment; and the organization remains accountable for the service model. Metadata should make the equivalent distinction between a human author and an organizational publisher.

## Governance check

A practical AI support governance model should show three things:

| Control | Expected behavior | Test equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | A named person owns judgment and escalation decisions | `author.name` and `author.url` identify Vince Lupe |
| Accountability | The operating organization remains responsible for the service | `publisher.name` identifies Callnovo Contact Center |
| Traceability | Customer and operational context persist through handoffs | Canonical URL, Open Graph metadata, image metadata, and FAQ schema agree on the same page identity |

This page is not a new market-facing article. It is a controlled rendering test derived from the prior AI customer support governance topic and intentionally limited to the components needed for technical validation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does this AI customer support governance test validate?

It validates that a Callnovo Contact Center Blog post can render a complete human author object for Vince Lupe, a distinct Callnovo Contact Center publisher object, a canonical page URL, social metadata, image metadata, and FAQPage structured data after a Jekyll configuration update.

### What should AI customer support governance define?

A governed AI customer support model should define which low-risk requests are automated, what confidence or risk conditions trigger human escalation, what context follows the escalation, and who is accountable for quality and exceptions.

### Why should author and publisher metadata be distinct?

The author identifies the accountable human responsible for the article, while the publisher identifies the organization responsible for the publication. Keeping them distinct improves metadata clarity and prevents a human byline from being incorrectly represented as the publishing organization.
