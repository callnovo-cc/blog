***Outcome-based pricing versus per-FTE staffing model comparison concept in a business setting.***

Outcome based pricing BPO models are gaining real, measurable ground in 2026 – not just as a talking point in vendor pitch decks.

Cognizant reports that 45% of its new BPO contracts are now signed under outcome-based commercial models, a sharp rise from prior years, as enterprises increasingly tie payment to measurable business results – rather than headcount or hours worked ([Moneycontrol, 2026](https://www.moneycontrol.com/shorts/artificial-intelligence/outcome-based-pricing-gains-ground-as-45-of-cognizant-s-new-bpo-contracts-adopt-the-model){:rel="noopener"}).

Everest Group frames this shift as the industry's move toward a new "value currency," in which providers are measured and paid against defined business outcomes – rather than labor inputs alone ([Everest Group, 2025](https://www.everestgrp.com/blogs/outcome-based-metrics-the-new-value-currency-in-bpo/){:rel="noopener"}).

For buyers, the practical question is not whether outcome-based pricing is trending – it clearly is – but whether it fits a given contract's actual work.

## Why Is Per-FTE Pricing Declining?

Per-FTE billing charges for staffed capacity whether or not that capacity is used efficiently, which was tolerable when labor was the only real input to a support operation.

Once AI can resolve a meaningful share of routine contacts at a lower marginal cost than a fully staffed FTE block, buyers reasonably begin asking why they should keep paying as though every solved issue required the same fixed amount of human labor, regardless of actual complexity.

## Outcome Based Pricing BPO Models Explained

Outcome-based pricing ties payment to a defined, measurable result – a resolved ticket, a completed transaction, a retained customer – rather than to hours staffed or seats filled.

Cognizant's 45% adoption figure for new contracts signals that this is now a mainstream commercial structure, not an experimental niche ([Moneycontrol, 2026](https://www.moneycontrol.com/shorts/artificial-intelligence/outcome-based-pricing-gains-ground-as-45-of-cognizant-s-new-bpo-contracts-adopt-the-model){:rel="noopener"}).

## Per resolution pricing vs per FTE: what actually changes?

Under per-FTE pricing, the buyer pays for staffed hours regardless of output. Under per-resolution pricing, the buyer pays only when a defined outcome is achieved – shifting performance risk from the buyer toward the provider, but only if "resolved" is defined tightly enough to prevent disputes.

## Why Cost-Per-Resolution Estimates Disagree So Much

Per-resolution rates vary widely across vendors because "resolution" itself is not a standardized term.

A resolution definition that excludes reopened cases, complex escalations, or multi-touch issues will always quote a lower per-resolution rate than one that counts every contact type equally – which is exactly why two vendor quotes with very different headline rates can represent the same underlying cost once scope is normalized.

## How to Evaluate a Vendor's Outcome-Pricing Offer

Before signing an outcome-based contract, buyers should get written, specific answers to:

- What precisely counts as "resolved" – and does that definition exclude any contact types?
- What happens when a case is reopened – does it count against the original resolution, or bill as a new one?
- Which issue types are explicitly excluded from the outcome rate and billed differently?
- How is quality measured on top of resolution volume, so the model doesn't reward fast-but-poor resolutions?

That single document will reveal more about true cost comparability than the headline per-resolution rate itself.

## FAQ

### Why is per FTE pricing declining?
Because it pays for staffed capacity rather than outcomes achieved. As automation improves and volume baselines mature, buyers increasingly want commercial models that reflect what actually gets solved, not just how many seats are staffed.

### How much does per resolution pricing cost?
It varies widely because a "resolution" can mean very different things across contracts. Always check complexity scope, reopening rules, and escalation treatment before comparing quoted per-resolution rates across vendors.

### Is outcome based pricing better for BPO contracts?
Sometimes. It works best when the underlying work is structured and measurable, which is why large providers like Cognizant now apply it to nearly half of new contracts. For highly judgment-heavy support, a pure outcome model can become distorted or difficult to govern fairly.

### What should a buyer negotiate into an outcome-based contract?
A precise, written definition of "resolved," clear rules for reopened cases, and an explicit list of issue types excluded from the outcome rate. These three items prevent most disputes that surface later in outcome-based engagements.
