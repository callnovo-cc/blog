***Outcome-based pricing versus per-FTE staffing model comparison concept in a business setting.***

Outcome based pricing BPO models are gaining real, measurable ground in 2026 – not just as a talking point in vendor pitch decks.

Cognizant reports that 45% of its new BPO contracts are now signed under outcome-based commercial models, a sharp rise from prior years, as enterprises increasingly tie payment to measurable business results – rather than headcount or hours worked ([Moneycontrol, 2026](https://www.moneycontrol.com/shorts/artificial-intelligence/outcome-based-pricing-gains-ground-as-45-of-cognizant-s-new-bpo-contracts-adopt-the-model)).

Everest Group frames this shift as the industry's move toward a new "value currency," in which providers are measured and paid against defined business outcomes – rather than labor inputs alone ([Everest Group, 2025](https://www.everestgrp.com/blogs/outcome-based-metrics-the-new-value-currency-in-bpo/)).

For buyers, the practical question is not whether outcome-based pricing is trending – it clearly is – but whether it fits a given contract's actual work.

**Why Is Per-FTE Pricing Declining?**

Per-FTE billing charges for staffed capacity whether or not that capacity is used efficiently, which was tolerable when labor was the only real input to a support operation.

Once AI can resolve a meaningful share of routine contacts at a lower marginal cost than a fully staffed FTE block, buyers reasonably begin asking why they should keep paying as though every solved issue required the same fixed amount of human labor, regardless of actual complexity.

HFS Research's long-running analysis of BPO commercial models notes that FTE-based pricing remains common for new engagements that lack a stable volume baseline, since a provider needs predictable revenue to justify staffing up for an unproven relationship.

But HFS also found that buyers increasingly push toward transaction or outcome-based structures once operations mature and volume patterns become well understood, because that maturity removes much of the risk that made FTE pricing attractive to providers in the first place ([HFS Research, 2022](https://www.hfsresearch.com/research/delivering-on-contractual-commitments-is-hygiene-but-you-get-what-you-pay-for/)).

Callnovo's homepage and pricing materials reflect this same shift by presenting both dedicated-team economics and solved-case economics side by side, rather than defaulting every client into one universal commercial model ([Callnovo, n.d.-b](https://callnovo.ai/); [Callnovo, n.d.-c](https://callnovo.ai/pricing/)).

**Outcome Based Pricing BPO Models Explained**

Outcome-based pricing charges against an agreed result – usually a resolved case – rather than time worked or seats staffed.

This sounds simple in principle – but the entire model depends on how precisely "resolved" is defined in the contract, since a loose definition invites disputes later.

**Per resolution pricing vs per FTE: what actually changes?**

Per-FTE pricing pays for time regardless of output; a fully staffed agent earns the same fee whether they resolve five contacts, or fifty in a shift.

Per-resolution pricing inverts this, paying for output regardless of time – a faster, more efficient resolution costs the same as a slower one, as long as both count as resolved under the contract's definition.

This inversion is exactly why outcome-based pricing appeals to buyers seeking efficiency gains: it aligns the vendor's financial incentive with speed and first-contact resolution, rather than with billable hours.

Callnovo's pay-per-resolution service page and its HeroDash structured-resolution article show how providers now segment this model in practice, treating structured, AI-resolvable contacts differently from higher-complexity human-handled work – rather than applying one flat resolution rate across all contact types ([Callnovo, n.d.-a](https://callnovo.ai/services/pay-per-resolution/); [Callnovo, 2026](https://callnovo.ai/blog/ai-customer-support-per-resolution-herodash/)).

That segmentation matters because a single blended per-resolution rate, applied indiscriminately, tends to either overpay for simple contacts or underpay for complex ones.

**Why Cost-Per-Resolution Estimates Disagree So Much**

How much does per resolution pricing cost?

The honest answer is that published estimates disagree widely, and that disagreement is not noise – it reflects real structural differences in how "resolved" gets defined across contracts.

Cost per resolution BPO depends on complexity, industry, automation depth, and crucially, whether a reopened case still counts as a single resolution or triggers a second billable event.

HFS Research's analysis of live finance-and-accounting and procurement BPO contracts found that nearly a quarter of new deals included some gain-sharing or pay-for-performance component, but the research cautioned that fairly apportioning credit for an outcome in a genuine partnership arrangement is difficult in practice, not just in theory ([HFS Research, 2022](https://www.hfsresearch.com/research/delivering-on-contractual-commitments-is-hygiene-but-you-get-what-you-pay-for/)).

Everest Group's outcome-metrics research makes a similar point from the buyer side: buyers and providers must jointly define and benchmark the right metrics before pricing can be set fairly, since a poorly specified resolution definition undermines the entire commercial model regardless of how attractive the headline rate looks ([Everest Group, 2025](https://www.everestgrp.com/blogs/outcome-based-metrics-the-new-value-currency-in-bpo/)).

A buyer who treats any single quoted cost-per-resolution figure as a universal benchmark is missing this risk entirely and is likely to be surprised by disputes over reopened cases, partial resolutions, or excluded issue types later in the contract term.

**How to Evaluate a Vendor's Outcome-Pricing Offer**

***Chart comparing per-FTE attendance coverage against per-resolution pricing tiers, with figures added as a text overlay.***

Before accepting an outcome-based quote, get specific answers to five questions:

1.  What exactly counts as a resolved case – does it require customer confirmation, or is it defined purely by agent or system status?
2.  What happens if the issue reopens within a defined window – does the provider absorb that cost, or does it trigger a new billable resolution?
3.  Are partial outcomes billed at a reduced rate, or does the contract only recognize full resolutions?
4.  Which issue types are explicitly excluded from the outcome-based rate and billed under a different structure instead?
5.  How are human-assisted exceptions priced when a contact starts as an AI-resolvable case but escalates partway through?

A vendor who answers these questions with contract language – rather than general reassurance – is offering a commercial structure a buyer can actually govern over the life of the relationship.

It also helps to pilot an outcome-based structure on a limited scope before rolling it out across an entire program.

A 90-day pilot on one contact type – say, order-status inquiries – surfaces disputes over resolution definitions and reopening rules while the stakes are still small, rather than discovering those gaps six months into a full-scale commercial commitment.

Vendors confident in their own resolution quality tend to welcome this kind of staged rollout, since it gives them a low-risk way to demonstrate performance before the relationship scales.

Callnovo's base commercial model remains per-FTE for judgment-heavy, recurring contact volume where dedicated staffing and consistent utilization make sense, while its HeroDash platform and pay-per-resolution service line support outcome-based billing specifically where a client's volume profile is structured enough to support it ([Callnovo, n.d.-d](https://callnovo.ai/herodash/); [Callnovo, n.d.-a](https://callnovo.ai/services/pay-per-resolution/)).

**Next Step**

Use contract clarity as the test – not pricing novelty.

A plain per-FTE model you can govern with confidence is often safer than an outcome-based model that sounds modern but cannot survive operational ambiguity once real contacts start flowing through it.

**FAQ**

**Question 1: Why is per FTE pricing declining?**  
Because it pays for staffed capacity rather than outcomes achieved. As automation improves and volume baselines mature, buyers increasingly want commercial models that reflect what actually gets solved, not just how many seats are staffed.

**Question 2: How much does per resolution pricing cost?**  
It varies widely because a "resolution" can mean very different things across contracts. Always check complexity scope, reopening rules, and escalation treatment before comparing quoted per-resolution rates across vendors.

**Question 3: Is outcome based pricing better for BPO contracts?**  
Sometimes. It works best when the underlying work is structured and measurable, which is why large providers like Cognizant now apply it to nearly half of new contracts. For highly judgment-heavy support, a pure outcome model can become distorted or difficult to govern fairly.

**Question 4: What should a buyer negotiate into an outcome-based contract?**  
A precise, written definition of "resolved," clear rules for reopened cases, and an explicit list of issue types excluded from the outcome rate. These three items prevent most disputes that surface later in outcome-based engagements.
