***Evaluating per-seat, per-resolution, and hourly pricing models for outsourced support.***

Contact center pricing models fall into a handful of recognizable structures:

- per-hour,
- per-FTE,
- per-transaction, and
- outcome-based.

Choosing the wrong one for your volume pattern is usually what makes outsourcing feel more expensive than it should.

Rate ranges also vary heavily by delivery location: 2026 industry data puts offshore agent rates around US$ 6.00 to US$ 14.00 per hour, nearshore around US$$ 9.00 to US$ 18.00, and US onshore around US$ 22.00 to US$ 45.00 ([StealthAgents, 2026](https://stealthagents.com/research/nearshore-bpo-cost-comparison)).

Understanding how these two variables – pricing structure and delivery geography – interact is the fastest way to evaluate whether a vendor's quote is actually competitive – or simply structured to look that way.

**Why Pricing Model Choice Matters More Than Headline Rate**

Buyers often compare vendors on hourly rate alone – but outsourced customer support cost is really a function of pricing structure plus delivery location plus contact complexity – not any single number in isolation.

A cheaper hourly rate attached to the wrong pricing model – say, per-FTE billing applied to highly variable, seasonal volume – can end up costing more in practice than a well-matched per-transaction structure priced at a nominally higher rate.

This is because per-FTE pricing charges for staffed capacity whether or not that capacity is fully utilized, while per-transaction and outcome-based models scale cost with actual work performed.

Callnovo's pricing page lays out this distinction directly, presenting multiple commercial structures rather than forcing every client into one universal rate card, precisely because different contact volume patterns genuinely call for different pricing logic ([Callnovo, n.d.-c](https://callnovo.ai/pricing/)).

Buyers who request a single number without first characterizing their own volume pattern are effectively asking a vendor to guess which model fits – a guess that tends to favor the vendor's margin, and not the buyer's actual cost efficiency.

**Contact Center Pricing Models Explained**

**What is per-FTE pricing?**

Per-FTE pricing charges a fixed monthly or hourly rate per dedicated agent, regardless of how many contacts that agent actually handles in a given period.

It works best for steady, predictable volume where staffing levels can be planned well in advance and utilization stays consistently high.

The risk is that if volume drops unexpectedly, the buyer still pays for full staffed capacity.

Callnovo's pricing page presents this structure alongside alternative commercial options specifically for clients whose volume profile supports dedicated, planned staffing ([Callnovo, n.d.-c](https://callnovo.ai/pricing/)).

Per-transaction pricing, by contrast, charges per resolved contact – a call, a chat, or a ticket – and suits variable or seasonal volume where a fixed staffing block would sit idle, and therefore be wasted cost, during slower periods.

This model shifts utilization risk from the buyer to the vendor, which is typically reflected in a somewhat higher per-unit rate than an equivalent per-FTE arrangement.

Outcome-based pricing ties payment to a defined result rather than either time worked or transaction volume, and works best on structured, easily measured workflows where "resolved" can be defined unambiguously in the contract.

Callnovo's pay-per-resolution service line applies this model specifically to contact types structured enough to support it ([Callnovo, n.d.-a](https://callnovo.ai/services/pay-per-resolution/)).

Most real engagements do not use exactly one of these models in isolation.

A support program handling both a steady baseline of account questions and a seasonal spike in order-status inquiries around major shopping periods might reasonably combine a per-FTE core team with per-transaction overflow capacity for the seasonal surge.

The pricing model itself is a tool for matching cost to actual work, not a fixed category a buyer must choose once and live with indefinitely – vendors who can flex between structures as a program matures are generally easier to work with than those who require a single model locked in from day one.

**How Much Does a Contact Center Cost in 2026?**

Call center pricing per hour varies sharply by delivery region, and this variance often matters more to total cost than the choice between pricing models.

Nearshore Latin American programs typically run US$ 9.00 to US$ 18.00 per agent hour – a meaningful discount to onshore US rates of US$ 22.00 to US$ 45.00 – while offshore destinations, such as the Philippines or India, run lower still, at roughly US$ 6.00 to US$ 14.00 per hour ([StealthAgents, 2026](https://stealthagents.com/research/nearshore-bpo-cost-comparison)).

CallForce Global's 2026 comparison of these three delivery tiers confirms similar bands and notes that nearshore keeps same-timezone coverage and typically stronger native-level English proficiency – advantages that offshore programs usually cannot match at an equivalent price point ([CallForce Global, 2026](https://callforce.global/blog/nearshore-vs-offshore-vs-onshore/)).

The risk in comparing vendors on headline hourly rate alone is that a lower per-hour figure can hide gaps elsewhere – reduced coverage hours, weaker escalation quality, and/or hidden transition and setup fees that only appear once a contract is signed.

A buyer evaluating three quotes at US$ 8.00, US$ 14.00, and US$ 20.00 per hour is not actually comparing three prices for the same service – but three different combinations of geography, coverage, and quality that happen to produce three different numbers.

Beyond geography, several other cost drivers routinely get left out of a headline rate quote.

Ramp-up and training costs for new agents are sometimes billed separately, absorbed into the rate, and vendors rarely volunteer which approach they use unless asked directly.

Technology and platform fees – for the dialer, CRM integration, or QA tooling – may be bundled into the hourly rate or billed as a separate line item, and the difference can meaningfully change the effective cost per hour.

Minimum volume commitments are another common lever: a vendor's advertised rate may only apply above a certain monthly contact volume, with a materially higher rate applying below that threshold.

Contract length and exit terms also affect the real cost of a pricing model, even though they rarely appear in a rate comparison.

A lower rate locked into a 24-month contract with steep early-termination penalties carries more risk than a modestly higher rate on a 6-month term with a clean exit clause, particularly for a buyer whose volume needs may shift.

None of this means lower rates are suspect by default; it means the rate alone is an incomplete basis for comparison, and a buyer should request a full cost breakdown – not just a per-hour number – before treating any two quotes as genuinely comparable.

**Matching a Pricing Model to Your Volume Pattern**
![Table comparing per-seat, per-resolution, per-minute, and cost-plus hourly pricing models, with figures added as a text overlay.]({{ '/assets/images/article-02-pricing-model-table.png' | relative_url }})

Steady, predictable volume with high, consistent utilization fits per-FTE pricing well, since the buyer captures the benefit of dedicated, trained staff without paying a variable-cost premium.

Seasonal or spiky volume fits per-transaction pricing better since it avoids paying for idle capacity during slow periods.

Structured, easily measured workflows – order status checks, appointment scheduling, simple account updates – fit outcome-based pricing, since "resolved" can be defined cleanly enough to price fairly.

Mixed contact types spanning all three patterns usually call for a hybrid commercial structure that blends a base FTE block for steady work with transaction or outcome pricing at the margins for variable and/or structured overflow.

**FAQ**

**Question 1: How much does a contact center cost?**  
It depends heavily on delivery location and pricing model chosen. In 2026, expect roughly US$ 6.00 to US$ 14.00 per hour offshore, US$ 9.00 to US$ 18.00 per hour nearshore, and US$ 22.00 to US$ 45.00 per hour for US onshore delivery.

**Question 2: What is per-FTE pricing?**  
A fixed rate charged per dedicated agent regardless of contact volume handled in a given period – well suited to steady, predictable workloads with consistently high utilization.

**Question 3: Which pricing model is best for customer support?**  
It depends on your volume pattern. Steady volume favors per-FTE pricing; variable or seasonal volume favors per-transaction pricing; structured, measurable workflows favor outcome-based pricing.

**Question 4: Why do hourly rates vary so much between vendors?**  
Rate differences usually reflect delivery geography, coverage hours, and escalation quality, not just margin. Comparing rates without accounting for these factors can lead to comparing fundamentally different services.
