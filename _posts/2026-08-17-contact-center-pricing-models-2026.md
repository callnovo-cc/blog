***Evaluating per-seat, per-resolution, and hourly pricing models for outsourced support.***

Contact center pricing models fall into a handful of recognizable structures:

- per-hour,
- per-FTE,
- per-transaction, and
- outcome-based.

Choosing the wrong one for your volume pattern is usually what makes outsourcing feel more expensive than it should.

Rate ranges also vary heavily by delivery location: 2026 industry data puts offshore agent rates around US$ 6.00 to US$ 14.00 per hour, nearshore around US$ 9.00 to US$ 18.00, and US onshore around US$ 22.00 to US$ 45.00 ([StealthAgents, 2026](https://stealthagents.com/research/nearshore-bpo-cost-comparison){:rel="noopener"}).

Understanding how these two variables – pricing structure and delivery geography – interact is the fastest way to evaluate whether a vendor's quote is actually competitive – or simply structured to look that way.

## Why Pricing Model Choice Matters More Than Headline Rate

A lower headline hourly rate can still produce a higher total cost of ownership if it comes bundled with a pricing structure mismatched to your actual volume pattern.

A per-FTE contract billed for steady, predictable capacity can quietly waste money during slow periods, while a per-transaction model applied to unpredictable, spiky volume can produce bill shock during peak season.

The useful diligence question is not "what is your rate," but "what pricing structure fits how our volume actually behaves."

## Contact Center Pricing Models Explained

Four structures dominate the market in 2026:

| Model | How it's billed | Best fit |
|---|---|---|
| Per-hour | Flat hourly rate per agent-hour staffed | Simple, short-term engagements |
| Per-FTE | Fixed monthly rate per dedicated agent | Steady, predictable, high-utilization volume |
| Per-transaction | Rate per contact, call, or ticket handled | Variable or seasonal volume |
| Outcome-based | Rate tied to a defined resolved outcome | Structured, measurable workflows |

## What is per-FTE pricing?

Per-FTE pricing charges a fixed rate per dedicated agent regardless of how many contacts that agent actually handles in a given period.

It works well when volume is steady and utilization stays consistently high, because the buyer is effectively pre-paying for guaranteed capacity rather than paying only for output.

It works poorly when volume dips, because the buyer keeps paying the same rate for underused capacity.

## How Much Does a Contact Center Cost in 2026?

Delivery geography remains the single biggest lever on cost.

2026 industry benchmarking puts offshore agent rates at roughly US$ 6.00 to US$ 14.00 per hour, nearshore at US$ 9.00 to US$ 18.00, and US onshore delivery at US$ 22.00 to US$ 45.00 per hour ([StealthAgents, 2026](https://stealthagents.com/research/nearshore-bpo-cost-comparison){:rel="noopener"}).

Those ranges reflect labor cost differences, but they also correlate with coverage hours, language capability, and escalation quality – which is why comparing two quotes purely on hourly rate can be misleading without checking what's actually included.

## Matching a Pricing Model to Your Volume Pattern

The practical decision rule is straightforward:

- Steady, predictable volume with high utilization favors per-FTE pricing.
- Variable or seasonal volume favors per-transaction pricing, so you are not paying for idle capacity.
- Structured, measurable workflows with a clear definition of "resolved" favor outcome-based pricing.

Buyers who mismatch model to volume pattern are the ones most likely to describe outsourcing as "more expensive than expected" a year into the contract.

## FAQ

### How much does a contact center cost?
It depends heavily on delivery location and pricing model chosen. In 2026, expect roughly US$ 6.00 to US$ 14.00 per hour offshore, US$ 9.00 to US$ 18.00 per hour nearshore, and US$ 22.00 to US$ 45.00 per hour for US onshore delivery.

### What is per-FTE pricing?
A fixed rate charged per dedicated agent regardless of contact volume handled in a given period – well suited to steady, predictable workloads with consistently high utilization.

### Which pricing model is best for customer support?
It depends on your volume pattern. Steady volume favors per-FTE pricing; variable or seasonal volume favors per-transaction pricing; structured, measurable workflows favor outcome-based pricing.

### Why do hourly rates vary so much between vendors?
Rate differences usually reflect delivery geography, coverage hours, and escalation quality, not just margin. Comparing rates without accounting for these factors can lead to comparing fundamentally different services.
